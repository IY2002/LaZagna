# Architectural Exploration of Two-Layer 3D FPGAs with LaZagna: Results

Status: **final.** Every number comes from the final single-synthesis-version results
(279 runs, 0 failures), generated by `campaign/analyze.py` into `docs/study/tables.md`. The
per-task result JSONs and the task list are versioned in `docs/study/results/` and
`docs/study/tasks_final.jsonl` (the working copies live in `campaign_work/study/`). Figures are in `docs/study/figs/`, assumptions in
STUDY_ASSUMPTIONS.md.

## Summary

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| At equal resources, a 2-layer FPGA reduces routed wirelength on every design: **+8.1% geomean**, 22/22 designs. CPD improves **+4.4% geomean** (17/22). | §2 |
| The 3D wirelength gain grows with the **hard-block share of the design's area** (Spearman +0.66). The biggest winners are BRAM/DSP-heavy designs: spmv +24% CPD / +16% WL, clstm +14% / +10%, bwave +12% / +16%. | §2 |
| **Lateral spread of hard blocks matters far more than their layer.** Clustering them costs 5–12% WL and 4–6% CPD. Staggering layers changes nothing. | §3 |
| **Type-dedicated layers (all DSP on one die, all BRAM on the other) shorten wires:** +1.4 to +5.8% WL on 6/6 designs at 10 seeds, CPD within noise, layer order irrelevant. An unconstrained layout search converges to the same arrangement. | §3 |
| **Thin vertical links per switch block, don't remove switch blocks.** At 10% of the links, cutting crossing channels costs −0.5% WL; cutting 3D switch blocks costs −5.7% WL / −5.7% CPD. Perimeter-only vertical links cost up to −24% WL. | §4 |
| **Recommended architecture** (type-dedicated layers, 10% of channels crossing at every switch block): **+4.0% CPD / +9.6% WL over 2D with 10× fewer vertical links**, vs +4.4% / +8.1% for fully connected 3D. | §5 |
| The **WL benefit is insensitive to vertical-link delay** (about +11% over 2D from 0.3× to 3× delay ratio). The **CPD benefit needs fast links**: it disappears around a 3× ratio. | §5 |

## 1. Method

**Architecture.** A VTR-style `dsp_bram` fabric:
- CLBs with 10 fracturable 6-LUTs;
- 4-row DSP blocks (one 27×27 or two 18×19 multipliers);
- 6-row single-port 20 Kb BRAMs (512×40, 1K×20 or 2K×10);
- a fixed column mix of 75% CLB, 12.5% DSP and 12.5% BRAM;
- channel width 300;
- LaZagna 3D switch blocks between two layers, with an inter-layer delay ratio of 0.739
  unless stated otherwise.

**Equal-resource comparison.** For each design, the 2D device is the smallest near-square
grid on which every block type is at most 80% used. The 3D device has two layers whose total
tile count matches the 2D device. Interior heights are multiples of 12, so tall blocks tile
exactly.

**Flow.**
- Yosys synthesis and VPR packing run once per design. The synthesis is LaZagna's flow with
  fixes for VTR primitives, ROMs mapped to BRAM, and single-write-port memories.
- The packed netlist is reused for every architecture variant. Routed results were verified
  bit-identical against the full per-run flow.
- Each configuration is placed (`cube_bb`) and routed with VPR on 5 fixed seeds (10 for
  confirmation runs). All comparisons are paired by seed.
- Metrics:
  - CPD is the final critical path delay; for multi-clock designs it is VPR's geomean
    intra-domain period.
  - WL is the routed wirelength.
  - Per-design gains are the mean ± 95% t-interval over seeds; cross-design summaries are
    geometric means.

**Benchmarks.** Koios (deep-learning, DSP/BRAM-heavy) and VTR designs, chosen by their
measured hard-block demand in a synthesis+pack characterization of 40 candidates. §6 lists
the exclusions and their reasons.

## 2. RQ1: 3D vs 2D at equal resources

22 designs, 5 paired seeds each (full table: `docs/study/tables.md`, figure
`figs/rq1_3d_vs_2d.png`).

- **Wirelength:** 3D is better on **22/22** designs, geomean **+8.1%** (range +1.7% blob_merge
  to +16.1% bwave). Most per-design intervals are ±1–3%.
- **CPD:** geomean **+4.4%**, better on 17/22. Large gains occur only on hard-block-heavy or
  sparse designs: spmv +23.8 ± 7.1%, clstm_like.small +13.9 ± 7.5%, bwave +11.7 ± 7.1%,
  tpu_like.small.os +7.3%, eltwise +7.1%, dla_like.small +5.0 ± 2.5%. Logic-dominated designs
  (bgm, blob_merge, LU8PEEng, LU32PEEng, softmax) are within ±1%.
- **What predicts the gain** (Spearman over designs): hard-block share of used area +0.66 (WL)
  / +0.42 (CPD); device size +0.54 / +0.32; CLB utilization −0.42 / −0.27.
- **Interpretation:** in 2D, tall DSP/BRAM columns force long lateral detours between logic
  and the hard blocks it uses. A second layer puts that logic within one vertical hop. Logic-
  dominated designs see only the generic shorter-distance benefit, which shows up in WL but
  not in CPD.

## 3. RQ2: hard-block placement across layers

Layouts keep the reference's exact block totals and are compared against evenly spread,
layer-aligned columns on the same 3D device (figures `figs/rq2_layouts_{cpd,wl}.png`).

- **Clustering is the dominant penalty.** Mean over designs: center −5.3% WL / −6.1% CPD;
  edges −11.9% / −4.2%; center on one layer with edges on the other −3.9% / −2.4%. Worst case
  is tpu_like.small.os with edges at −27.2% WL. The only exception is arm_core (no DSPs,
  BRAM-only), where edge placement helps (+4.9% WL).
- **Vertical arrangement barely matters.** Staggering layer 1 by half a pitch: +0.1% WL /
  −0.7% CPD.
- **Type-dedicated layers help WL.** DSP on layer 0 and BRAM on layer 1 averages +1.3% WL over
  22 designs at 5 seeds (significant on bwave +6.1, mkDelayWorker +6.6, stereovision2 +4.3,
  conv +2.0, dla +1.2). At **10 seeds**, it is better on **6/6** designs: +1.4 to +5.8% WL
  (CI ±0.7–1.5), with layer order irrelevant (BRAM-first: +2.1 to +4.8%). The CPD effect is
  within noise (−2.1 to +0.8%), with a slight negative lean.
- **All hard blocks on one layer (a logic-only die) is not free.** At 10 seeds, WL is −2.1 to
  +4.4% and CPD −1.0 to −5.9% (eltwise significant at −5.9 ± 2.5%). Separating hard-block
  *types* by layer is safe; separating hard blocks from *logic* costs timing on some designs.
- **The layout search agrees.** A 5-parameter Optuna search (routed, 3 seeds, about 45 trials
  per design) found the strongest negative correlate of WL gain was clustering in every design
  (Spearman −0.27, −0.57, −0.79). Its best layouts put most BRAM on one layer and most DSP on
  the other (e.g. BRAM 15–16 of 16 and DSP 4–5 of 16 on layer 0 for tpu). Re-run at 5 seeds on
  the final flow they give +0.1 to +2.3% WL, consistent with, and not better than, plain type
  separation.
- **Mechanism (interpretation):** with one type per layer, each layer carries twice as many
  columns of that type at half the pitch, so logic on either layer is laterally closer to a DSP
  (on one die) and a BRAM (on the other), each one vertical hop away.

## 4. RQ3: vertical connectivity vs cost

Two ways to reduce the number of vertical links (figure `figs/rq3_connectivity.png`):
make only a fraction **p** of switch blocks 3D, or keep every switch block 3D and let only a
fraction **v** of its channels cross. Change vs full connectivity, mean over 6 designs:

| vertical links kept | fewer 3D switch blocks (CPD / WL) | fewer crossing channels (CPD / WL) |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | −2.2% / −0.5% | −0.6% / +0.0% |
| 25% | −2.7% / −1.8% | −1.2% / −0.2% |
| 10% | **−5.7% / −5.7%** | **−2.1% / −0.5%** |
| 5% | – | −0.7% / −2.7% |

- **For a fixed link budget, spread thin links everywhere.** Mixed strategies at 10% fall in
  between (p 0.5 × v 0.2 and p 0.2 × v 0.5); WL loss grows as links become more concentrated.
- With every switch block 3D and only 5–10% of channels crossing, almost all of the 3D WL
  benefit remains (the 2D device is 5–18% worse in WL on these designs). That is a 10–20×
  reduction in inter-die connections (micro-bumps, TSVs or hybrid-bond pads).
- **Where the 3D switch blocks go matters** (p = 0.25). Even interleaving (default) costs
  −0.3 to −2.7% WL; core-only −1.8 to −6.2%; rows or columns −0.6 to −5.2%; **perimeter-only
  −4.2 to −24.3% WL, with CPD up to −48%**. Vertical links belong spread across the fabric's
  core, not concentrated at its edges.

## 5. A recommended 3D architecture

Combining RQ2 and RQ3: DSP and BRAM on separate layers, every switch block 3D, and only 10%
of channels crossing (10× fewer vertical links than full connectivity). Geomean gain over the
equal-resource 2D device, 22 designs (figure `figs/recommended_vs_2d.png`):

| architecture | CPD | WL | vertical links |
|---|---|---|---|
| full 3D, aligned hard blocks | +4.4% | +8.1% | 100% |
| type-dedicated layers | +3.9% | +9.4% | 100% |
| **recommended** (type-dedicated + v = 0.1) | **+4.0%** | **+9.6%** | **10%** |

**Inter-layer delay sensitivity** (6 designs, aligned 3D, mean gain over 2D; figure
`figs/delay_sensitivity.png`): the WL gain is flat at about +11% for delay ratios 0.3 → 3.0.
The CPD gain is +6.9% (0.3), +7.1% (0.739), +4.1% (1.5) and **−2.5% (3.0)**. The recommended
architecture follows the same trend. Wire savings are robust to the bonding technology; the
timing advantage depends on vertical links being no slower than about 1.5× an L4 wire driver.

## 6. Limitations and threats to validity

- One architecture family: one CLB, one routing architecture, one channel width, two layers.
- Per-design CPD seed noise is ±4–6% (95% CI); the WL conclusions are much tighter (±1–3%).
- **Memory-mapping approximations:** write ports are merged, and the BRAM read enable is
  ignored. This changes some designs' functional behavior but not their routing structure.
  Asynchronous-read memories stay in soft logic, because the architecture has no LUTRAM.
- **Excluded designs:**
  - gemm_layer: about 7 h per routed configuration (pre-final RQ1 result only);
  - lenet: synthesis-flow bug found late (enable FFs left after the last techmap); fix applied
    to yosys.ys after the final run, untested;
  - tdarknet_like.small: 3005 BRAMs require a 455×398 device;
  - boundtop and tpu.16x16.int8: multiply-driven nets in the source;
  - ch_intrinsics: benchmark bug (undeclared RAM enables);
  - matmul_8x8_fp16: needs a floating-point MAC hard block;
  - tiny or IO-bound designs whose 3D device can't match the 2D tile count.
- The structured Optuna search ran on an earlier synthesis version; its top layouts are
  re-validated on the final flow.
